About Us


What does The Omelas Times mean? Where is Omelas?

The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas is a short story by Ursula K. Le Guim. It is only four pages long and well worth a read, it's available for free here.


The TL;DR for those who have not taken a moment to read a short story to understand the name of the website (entirely reasonable), is that the book describes a utopia. The maintenance of this utopia, however, requires that a single child suffer perpetually, for the continued happiness of the rest of the society. Should the child be rescued from this horrible fate, the utopia would collapse in on itself. Most people make their peace with this, and accept that the benefits of living in utopia comes at the expense of a suffering child. Some people decide that they cannot tolerate this, and become the titular ones who walk away.

Outside of this fictional world, we do not live in utopia. A handful of very rich people, however, have built something like it for themselves. And it takes the suffering of far more than one child to sustain it.


What is the point of the website?

The goal of the Omelas Times is to shine a spotlight on the suffering, and the extreme wealth, and to explain the causal link between them. We believe that most people would not be willing to tolerate a world where many people suffer for a few people to become still richer, if they understood the problem in its entirety.

Most people do not understand, though. This is in part because mainstream media is owned by the ultra-wealthy. You will not read in the Washington Post about Amazon workers, not allowed to take toilet breaks, peeing in bottles, all in service of making Jeff Bezos just slightly richer. Because Jeff Bezos owns the Washington Post. News that wishes to report on abuses by the rich and powerful cannot function if it is owned by the rich and powerful, and so independant newspapers must exist.


So you think the problem is that Jeff Bezos is evil?

We believe that the issue is not with individuals, but with systems. Imagine that Jeff Bezos decided to treat all of his workers fairly, paid them properly, and did not work them overly hard. He now risks being outcompeted by someone who does exploit their workers. If Jeff Bezos had set out deciding to treat his workers well, Amazon would have lost to one of its early competitors, and we would simply be talking about a different individual presiding over the same exploitation.

The problem here is not the individuals who have taken advantage of the system. They may have exploited others, but the system ensured that the exploitation would exist. They just decided to be the ones who would profit. As such, the solution is not to change the individuals, but the system.


What are you, some sort of socialist?

Yes.


Let's say I agree with your argument. I'm just one person. What am I supposed to do about it?

This is a common concern, if the problem is so large, what can one person even do. The answer is that systemic problems require systemic solutions, and that those solutions are produced by groups of individuals like yourself working collectively. We've built a guide on what you can do, titled How to Walk Away From Omelas, but please note that we do not have all the answers. It's up to you to try to understand the problem, and do what you think best to solve it.


How much are you being paid?

The Omelas Times does not make any money. If, in future, that changes, 100% of it would be donated to charity. We are not in this to make the world better, not to make money.


How is the Omelas Times run?

Currently, the Omelas Times consists of one woman writing the articles, wrestingly with the html and css to try to get the website to work, and posting the articles to social media. Should you wish to contribute an article, or help with/improve the website, or anything of that nature, please get in touch via our Contact Us page. You will be properly credited, or not, as you prefer


What is your policy on the use of AI?

We do not use AI. All of our articles are 100% human written, any errors therein are our own. If you think I sound a little like AI, unfortunately for both of us, this is just how I write.
